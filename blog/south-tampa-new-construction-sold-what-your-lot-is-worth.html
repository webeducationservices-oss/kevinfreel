Another South Tampa New Construction Home, Sold

Kevin Freel August 20, 2026 7 min read

3223 W. Paxton Ave. is sold.

Five bedrooms, four baths, custom built by BCL Homes, on a South Tampa street that looked very different a couple of years ago. I represented the seller on this one, and it moved the way a well built home in the right pocket of South Tampa tends to move.

But the house is only half the story. The other half is the lot it sits on, and what that lot was before this home existed. If you own an older home in South Tampa, that part matters more to you than the finishes do.

What Sold: A Custom BCL Homes Build

This was not a flip and it was not a builder grade spec house. BCL builds a real custom product, and buyers in this price range can tell the difference within about ninety seconds of walking through the front door.

5 bedrooms, 4 full baths
Open main level built for how families actually live
Primary suite that reads like the reason you bought the house
High end finishes throughout, not just in the kitchen
Oversized backyard with room for a pool
Under 10 minutes to Downtown Tampa and Hyde Park Village

That last one does more work than people expect. South Tampa buyers are not only buying square footage. They are buying the drive to work, the drive to dinner, and the drive to school. A big backyard this close in is genuinely hard to find, and it is usually the thing that turns a maybe into an offer.

Two Open Houses in One Weekend, and the Buyers Came to Both

Most agents hold one open house on a weekend. I held two on this listing, Saturday and Sunday.

There is a reason for that. A new construction home is a lot to absorb in one visit. The first walkthrough is emotional, people are reacting to light and space and the feel of the place. The second walkthrough is where the real questions come out. How does the laundry work. Where would the pool go. Is that closet as big as I remember.

The buyers on this home came through both days. By Sunday afternoon they were not touring anymore, they were measuring. When somebody comes back a second time, they have usually already decided. My job at that point is to make it easy for them to say yes.

When the Buyer Brings an Attorney Instead of an Agent

Here is where this deal got interesting, and where I think a newer agent would have run into trouble.

The buyers were not working with a real estate agent. They were working with an attorney.

That is not a problem, and it is not unusual at higher price points, but it changes the shape of the transaction. In a normal deal there is an agent on the other side who moves the process along, chases their own client for signatures, explains what an inspection period is, and keeps everyone pointed at the closing date. When that person is not there, the listing agent absorbs a lot of it.

The negotiation itself read differently too. An attorney is protecting their client's legal position, which is exactly what they should be doing. It is a different conversation than the one you have with another agent, and if you have not done it before it can feel like the deal is going sideways when it is actually going fine.

Everything else was normal. Standard inspections. Builder warranties, which on a new build are a real part of the value and worth walking a buyer through carefully. It was more work on my end than a typical transaction, and that is fine. That is the job, and this builder and this seller are my clients.

I have been doing this in Tampa Bay since 1985. Forty years in, most of what I get paid for is not opening doors. It is knowing what to do when a deal does not follow the usual script.

What Your South Tampa Teardown Lot Is Actually Worth

Now the part that probably brought you here.

This home replaced an older house. That is how most South Tampa new construction happens now, because there is very little raw land left inside the good pockets. Builders are not finding empty lots. They are buying somebody's house, taking it down, and building.

Which means if you own an older home in South Tampa, you may be sitting on something a builder wants. Two things drive that number more than anything else.

1. The Lot Itself

Dimensions decide what can be built, and what can be built decides what a builder can pay. A lot that supports the home a builder wants to put up is worth real money. A lot that forces compromises, an awkward width, a shallow depth, a shape that eats into the buildable footprint, is worth meaningfully less even one street over.

This is also why the condition of your existing house matters far less than most sellers assume. If the house is coming down, a renovated kitchen does not move the number much. I have talked more than one seller out of spending money on a home right before selling it to a builder.

2. Where That Lot Sits

Two lots with identical dimensions can be worth very different numbers depending on the block. The street, the schools that serve it, what has already been built nearby, and how close you are to the places people actually want to be all feed into what a builder will pay.

Builders are underwriting the finished home, not your lot in isolation. They are asking what a new construction home sells for on that specific street. If recent builds nearby have done well, your lot is worth more. It really is that direct.

Why Builders Keep Calling

I sell most of BCL Homes' South Tampa builds. That is an ongoing relationship, not a one time listing, and it exists because new construction is genuinely different from resale.

A builder needs someone who can price a home that has no comparable history, explain a warranty package without hedging, and manage a buyer through selections and timelines without losing them halfway through. Get that wrong and a great home sits.

I work in the over one million dollar part of this market regularly, and I have handled deals with plenty of moving parts, including helping Kenan Thompson buy a home here in Tampa Bay. What that adds up to is simple: when something unusual shows up in a transaction, I have almost certainly seen a version of it before.
