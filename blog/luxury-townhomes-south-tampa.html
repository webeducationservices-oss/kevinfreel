Luxury Townhomes in South Tampa:
A Guide for Buyers and Sellers

Kevin Freel October 8, 2026 9 min read

There is no land left in South Tampa. When a builder wants to put up something new here, they buy a 1,300 square foot house, tear it down and build up. That is why the townhome became South Tampa’s luxury product, and why nearly every kind of buyer I work with is looking at one.

I have been selling South Tampa real estate since 1985, and I have been on both sides of these homes: helping buyers into them, selling them for owners, and helping turn an old teardown into three of them. This guide is in two halves. Buyers first, because the questions that matter most are the ones people forget to ask. Then sellers, because the townhome you buy today is competing with the one a builder finishes next door in a few years.

I’m buying ↓ I’m selling ↓

What Makes a South Tampa Townhome Luxury

Every new townhome listing seems to use the word. Here is where I actually draw the line: at about $900,000 is when you hit luxury. Below that you are buying quality new construction. Above it, you are buying size, finishes and an address.

Mid-range townhome 1,400 to 1,500 sq ft
Luxury townhome 2,500 sq ft and up
Where luxury starts About $900,000
Where builders try to stop $990,000

Size is the big feature. Luxury here means 2,500 square feet and up, in a good school zone. The top of the market is the three-story townhome, and once you add that third floor you can pass $1 million quickly. Builders know it, so they work hard to hold the price at $990,000 and keep it under seven figures. If you notice a lot of new townhomes listed at exactly $990K, that is not a coincidence.

Past size, it gets personal. Some buyers want a private pool. Some want a rooftop terrace. Finishes matter at this price, from the counters to the appliance package, and buyers today expect quartz rather than granite.

It Comes Down to the Land

Because there is no empty land, every new luxury townhome in South Tampa replaced something. The best example I can give you is one I was part of from the beginning.

At 1206 W Lemon Street in North Hyde Park, a longtime investor client of mine bought a dated teardown that had five different owners attached to a single parcel. Getting the lot rezoned for townhomes took months of planning, applications and community meetings. When the approvals came through, we matched it with a builder I trust, and the old house became three luxury townhomes.

1206 W Lemon: a quartz kitchen island, the primary bath, the first-floor guest suite with its own bathroom, and the open main living level.

Each one is what I mean by luxury in this market:

4 bedrooms, 3.5 baths, 2,412+ square feet
Private two-car garage
First-floor guest suite with its own bath and walk-in closet
Quartz counters, custom cabinetry and engineered hardwood
High-impact, hurricane-rated windows
Private outdoor living space

When we listed them, they were the only new four-bedroom townhomes priced in the low $900Ks in North Hyde Park, zoned for Gorrie Elementary, Wilson Middle and Plant High. That is the luxury line in practice: right at $900,000, with the space and the schools to justify it.

Who Is Buying, and What They Pay For

Every demographic likes South Tampa. I regularly work with all of these buyers:

Downsizers and empty nesters. Leaving a big South Tampa house, but not the neighborhood.
Young professionals and couples. New construction, low maintenance, and dinner within walking distance.
Families. The school zone and the space, at a price below a comparable single-family home.
Buyers relocating from out of state. Coming from markets where this kind of home costs far more.

What they pay for depends on who they are. Families buy the school zone. Most everyone else buys walkability and proximity: walking to Hyde Park Village or SoHo, being close to Bayshore, and a short drive downtown.

North Hyde Park sits minutes from downtown Tampa. Proximity like this is what most luxury townhome buyers are really paying for.

Two pockets a few blocks apart can price very differently. If you are still deciding where to look, I wrote up all 52 South Tampa neighborhoods, including what each one feels like to live in.

Which South Tampa Townhomes Come With a Two-Car Garage?

At the luxury level in South Tampa, you will find either a one-car or a two-car attached garage. You will not see tandem garages, where one car parks behind the other, or detached garages in this tier.

If parking matters to you, and it should, the two-car garage is worth holding out for. It is also one of the features that matters most when you sell, which I come back to below. The townhomes at 1206 W Lemon have private two-car garages. If you want to know which builders are building right now and what their plans include, I keep a current list of Tampa townhome builders.

Think About Logistics, Not Just How Nice It Is

I would not call this a mistake, but it is the thing I see most: buyers fall for how nice the house is, and they do not think about the logistics. That is part of my job. Before you write an offer, walk through these:

Parking. A one-car garage and one driveway spot becomes a problem fast with two cars and a guest. Is there street parking on the block?
Entertaining. If you host twelve people, where do they park, and where do they actually gather?
Storage. There is usually no attic and no shed. Where do the bikes, the holiday decorations and the golf clubs go?
Outdoor space. How much private yard, courtyard or rooftop do you really get, and is it enough for how you live?

The logistics: garages off a rear drive, a walk-in closet with built-ins, laundry on the bedroom level, and the parking court out back.

One more thing to check before you are committed: whether the home is legally a townhome or a condo. Two buildings on the same street can look identical and still sit under different rules, and it changes your down payment. I explained it in Townhome or Condo in South Tampa?

Buy Right, and It Pays You Back

Here is the story I tell buyers who worry about paying South Tampa prices.

A past client, a bachelor, bought a two-bedroom, two-and-a-half-bath Hyde Park townhome with me for $275,000. It had a one-car garage. About six and a half years later he called and asked me to sell it.

We listed it at $450,000. Within 30 minutes of it going live, six agents had called me. I showed it seven times that night between 4 and 8, and more than 25 more times that Friday. We received 14 offers, and it sold for $45,000 over asking, in cash. He bought it for $275,000 and sold it for $495,000.

That was the spring 2022 market, and it will not repeat every year. The part that does repeat is the lesson: location carried a one-car townhome to 14 offers. Read the original post from that week.

For Sellers: What Holds Value

When a South Tampa townhome goes back on the market, location is always the biggest factor. You cannot change it, and it is why buying right matters so much. After location, these are what buyers pay for when you sell:

Age. Newer resells better. Buyers compare everything to the new construction down the street.
A reasonable HOA. A fee that is clearly worth what it covers helps. A high one with little to show for it hurts.
A two-car garage. A big plus at resale. The parking question every buyer forgot when they bought is the first one the next buyer asks.
Width. Wider units simply feel better when you walk in.
End units. More windows, more light, and usually more outdoor space.

Competing With the New Build Down the Street

Because newer resells better, the hardest listing in South Tampa is a six- to ten-year-old townhome with brand-new construction going up on the same street. You will not win by pretending yours is new. You win on how it shows and on price.

Neutral and modern. The interior needs to look current, with finishes a buyer would choose today.
Dark wood is out. Dark wood will kill a resale.
Quartz over granite. Buyers expect stone, and quartz is what reads current right now.
Price. Price always helps. Priced right, an older townhome is the value next to the new build.

Sometimes getting a townhome ready is more than paint. A seller in California once called me after receiving one of my Just Sold postcards. Their Hyde Park townhome, a courtyard unit with no HOA fees, had an air conditioner leak that caused mold. I brought in my own vendors for drywall, paint, plumbing, wood flooring, trim and a new AC system, and the townhome was like new again. It sold for $35,000 over asking. Read how that one came together.

How I Market a Luxury Townhome

Every one of these matters, and on the best sales they all work together:

Professional photography, video and drone. Buyers decide online whether to come see it, so the listing has to win there first.
Open houses. On new construction like 3223 W Paxton, I held two in one weekend, and the buyers came to both.
My network. Past clients, agents who call me first, and the Just Sold postcards that brought in the courtyard seller above.
Pricing to create competition. The right price brings several buyers in at once. That is how one townhome drew 14 offers.

You can see the full approach in my marketing plan and the listing photography I use.
