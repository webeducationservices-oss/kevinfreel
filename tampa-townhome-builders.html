Who Actually Builds Them

Tampa Townhome Builders

Every townhome in this directory came from one of these builders. These are the names Kevin Freel has sold work from since Tampa townhomes started going up, what each one is known for, where they build, and what to ask before you sign anyone's contract.

The Short Version

Why the Builder's Name on the Permit Matters

Two Tampa townhomes on the same block, same price, same square footage, can be a very different ten-year ownership. The builder decides finish quality, warranty length, how calls get answered after you close, and how an upgrade conversation actually goes. Most Tampa townhome builders will not deal directly with a homeowner, which is a good reason to have an agent who already does.

Recent South Tampa new construction Kevin has sold or represented. Nothing here is a stock photo.

The Directory

Seven Builders Active Right Now

Alphabetical except for the most active at the top. Everyone listed below has either built product Kevin has sold or dealt with his office on new sites.

Most prolific infill

Mobley Custom Homes

Mobley is the most-named builder on this site. Custom homes with many floor plans tuned to small 50 by 100 Tampa infill lots, which is almost every Tampa Heights, Riverside Heights and inner South Tampa site. The standard plan is honest; most buyers add through the upgrade list.

What to watch for: upgrade list length. The delta from the standard spec to the model-home finish is where the number moves.
Mobley project with Kevin mobleyhomes.com

Design-forward partners

Momentum Homes

Two partners who put real detail into every unit. Their modern farmhouse look is what buyers show Kevin in the "I want this" photo. Momentum built the four custom townhomes at 4324 W Gray Street in South Tampa.

What to watch for: get the finish schedule in writing. Momentum's style is tempting; make sure the contract matches the showing.
4 Momentum townhomes at W Gray momentumhometampa.com

South Tampa custom

Starling Custom Homes

Starling built 3223 W Paxton Avenue in South Tampa with BCL Homes, the newer end of what single-lot custom product looks like near Hyde Park Village. Kevin represented the seller on that one and ran both open houses.

What to watch for: on a one-off custom like Paxton, the builder’s warranty and the subcontractor list matter more than they do on a bigger project.
3223 Paxton open house Starling at Paxton (first write-up) starlingcustomhomes.com

Premium one-off builds

BCL Homes

One of the most-mentioned builders on this site in its own right, and Starling’s partner on 3223 W Paxton Avenue. The right call for premium one-off builds near Hyde Park, Beach Park and Bayshore.

What to watch for: lot size drives the plan. Ask early which of their plans actually fit the lot you are looking at.
3223 Paxton open house bclhomes.com

Modern on your lot

State Street Homes

Modern-style builder who will build on your lot. The right call if you already own the land and want contemporary rather than modern farmhouse.

What to watch for: flat and low-slope roofs cost less to build and more to insure. Get a real quote before you sign.

Boutique custom

Tamlin Homes

Smaller-volume custom builder Kevin has written about on several South Tampa projects, including sophisticated pool homes with the owner's retreat on the first floor. A good fit when you want the custom look without the biggest-builder price tag.

What to watch for: timeline. Boutique builders are detail-first; expect that to show up in the schedule.

The Conversation

Seven Questions for Any Tampa Townhome Builder

The sales office is paid to answer the easy ones. These are the ones that save money.

  • WARRANTYWhat is covered, for how long, and who answers the phone in year two? Builders differ more here than they let on.
  • HOACan I see the current HOA documents, what does the fee cover, and can it rise? On some Tampa townhomes there is no HOA at all, which is a different value calculation.
  • FINISH SCHEDULEWhat is in the base plan versus the model home? Get it on paper. This is where surprise costs live.
  • INSURANCEHave you had buyers get a real quote on this build, or just an estimate? Flood zone and elevation still move the number on new construction.
  • SUBCONTRACTORSWho did the roof, the stucco, the plumbing on the last three projects? Lower subs change over time; a builder who knows this answer is paying attention.
  • SCHEDULEWhat are you actually hitting on your current builds, and what are you doing if you miss my date?
  • REFERENCESBuyers from the last two projects. Not reviews. Real phone numbers, with permission.
