For Townhome Buyers

New Townhomes in Tampa

Where they are being built right now, what they cost, and the questions worth asking before you sign on a home that does not exist yet. Kevin has sold new construction townhomes across Tampa since they started going up.

The Trade

Why Townhomes Took Over Tampa

Land close to downtown ran out before demand did. A single lot that once held one older house now holds two, three, sometimes four townhomes, which is the only way builders can deliver a brand new home this close in at a price a normal buyer can reach.

What you are really trading is yard for location. You give up the big backyard and you get new construction, a warranty, low maintenance, and a five to ten minute drive to work and dinner instead of thirty. For a lot of buyers, particularly first-time buyers and people downsizing out of a large South Tampa house, that is the right trade.

The Map

Where They Are Actually Going Up

Townhome construction in Tampa is concentrated in a handful of pockets, and they are not interchangeable. Each one attracts a different buyer and prices differently.

1

Tampa Heights

The busiest townhome corridor in the city, and the one most people mean when they search. Walkable to Armature Works and the Riverwalk, and close enough to downtown to skip the commute entirely. New product keeps arriving here.

2

Seminole Heights & Riverside Heights

A little further out, a little more house for the money, and a strong neighbourhood identity. Buyers who want new construction without downtown pricing tend to land here.

3

Ybor & Palmetto Beach

The value end of new construction close to the core. Still emerging, which means more upside and more variation block to block than the Heights.

4

Hyde Park & South Tampa

The luxury end. Fewer units, higher finishes, and prices that reflect the address. Some come with no HOA fee at all, which is rarer than buyers expect.

5

Westshore Marina District

Waterfront-adjacent and master-planned, so the feel is different from an infill build on a single lot. Amenities carry more of the value here.

The Numbers

What They Cost

Ranges move with the market, so treat these as orientation rather than a price list. Kevin can give you today's number for a specific building.

Entry new construction, further from downtown: high $300s to mid $400s
The Heights and Ybor corridors: mid $400s to high $600s
Hyde Park and South Tampa luxury: $650k and up, some well past $1M
Waterfront and master-planned: priced on amenities, not just square footage

Two buildings on the same street can price very differently depending on the builder, the finish level, and whether there is an HOA carrying real amenities. Square footage alone will mislead you here more than it does with single family homes.

Before You Sign

Five Things to Check on Any New Townhome

Buying new construction is not the same transaction as buying a resale, and the contract is usually the builder's, not the standard one.

The HOA, in writing. What the fee covers, what it does not, and whether it can rise. On some Tampa townhomes there is no HOA at all, which is worth real money.
The builder's warranty. What is covered, for how long, and who answers the phone in year two. This is a genuine part of the value and it varies a lot between builders.
Insurance, before you commit. Get a real quote rather than an estimate. New construction usually insures well, but flood zone and elevation still decide the number.
What is standard and what is an upgrade. The model you walked through is rarely the one in your contract. Get the finish schedule in writing.
Parking and storage, honestly. Tandem garages and tight alleys are common in infill townhomes. Walk it before you sign, not after.
Who represents you. The agent in the sales office works for the builder. Bringing your own agent costs you nothing and changes who is looking out for your side.
