The part listings skip

Buyers Fall for How Nice It Is. I Check How It Lives.

Every new townhome looks great in the photos. What decides whether you love it a year later is logistics, and that is what I walk through with you before you ever write an offer.

Parking

A one-car garage and one driveway spot gets tight fast with two cars and a guest. Is there street parking on the block?

Entertaining

If you host twelve people, where do they park, and where do they actually gather?

Storage

Usually no attic and no shed. Where do the bikes, the holiday decorations and the golf clubs go?

Outdoor space

How much private yard, courtyard or rooftop do you really get, and is it enough for how you live?

The HOA

What it covers, what it does not, and whether the home is legally a townhome or a condo, which changes your down payment.

Resale

Location first, then age, a reasonable HOA, a two-car garage, width and end units. I tell you how each one stacks up.

What your budget buys

Where Luxury Starts in South Tampa

Here is where I draw the line: about $900,000. Mid-range townhomes run 1,400 to 1,500 square feet. Luxury is 2,500 square feet and up in a good school zone, and at this level the garage is one or two cars, attached.

The townhomes at 1206 W Lemon in North Hyde Park are a good example. I was part of that project from the teardown on: four bedrooms, 2,412+ square feet, a private two-car garage, quartz kitchens and a first-floor guest suite, listed in the low $900Ks.
